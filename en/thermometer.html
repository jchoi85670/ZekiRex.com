AI DEMAND THERMOMETER · AS OF Q2 2026 · PROTOTYPE

ZekiRex™ AI Demand Thermometer

Q2 2026: money 97 (Overheated) · chips 90 (Overheated) · power 30 (Cooling). Money and chips are overheated; power is the one part not keeping up.

We read the three gauges separately rather than as one number: averaging lets one gauge's extreme be buried by the others (the average, for reference, is 72).

What this thermometer is for
  1. How hot AI demand is right now, split into three gauges on the same 0–100 scale: money (investment), chips (AI chip and cloud revenue) and power (data-center construction).
  2. Which part cools first. Whether all three rise together, or one lags, shows where demand is bottlenecked or cracking.
  3. Not missing the turn in the chip and memory cycle. Cooling signals are recorded every quarter under the same rules, and whether they were right is scored in public.
What it does not do: forecast next quarter, or name stocks or when to buy or sell. Over the past 10 quarters the reading did not call next-quarter memory prices or stocks in advance.

Track record — when each gauge was at an extreme (long history, proxy data)

The AI-specific numbers start in 2023. We rebuilt the same structure from official statistics going back to the 2000s and counted how semiconductor stocks did against the market after each gauge hit an extreme. The clear patterns: semis were strong after money cooled and after chips cooled. But the 11 money-cool quarters are only three cycles (2009, 2020, 2023); chips about nine. "Weak after hot" did not show up; after money ran hot, the beat rate (64%) was about the baseline.

Rules (fixed)

  • Money, chips and power are each shown on their own 0–100 percentile scale. Their average is given for reference only.
  • Each indicator uses year-over-year growth, converted to a percentile against a fixed base window, Q1 2024–Q2 2026. The base window will not be changed later.
  • Efficiency shock (doing the same work with fewer resources) is shown as an alert, not a number.
  • Bands: overheated 80+ · hot 60–80 · normal 40–60 · cooling 20–40 · cold under 20. Arrows compare with the previous quarter.
  • Watched alongside: memory stocks' strength relative to the S&P 500. In all four past DRAM cycles it turned at or before the price peak.

Scoring — the average over the last 10 quarters

Over the past 10 quarters, correlations between the reading and next-quarter memory measures were all near zero or weakly negative. Ten points cannot tell signal from chance.

ComparisonCorrelation
Reading → next-quarter DRAM contract price change−0.28

How to read this: with 10 data points, a correlation must exceed ±0.63 before it can be told apart from chance (5% significance). All four values are less than half that. The data so far could not confirm a relationship — which is not proof that none exists.

Of 16 quarters of DRAM contract prices, only 2 are actuals; the rest are TrendForce forecast midpoints. The whole period was an upcycle in which demand never cooled. The first public scoring comes after all Q3 2026 results are out (early November), on this page and in the weekend edition.

Limits

  • This is a prototype. Some figures come from secondary sources (stockanalysis.com, Our World in Data) and are being checked against company releases. Anything that changes will be corrected, and the correction noted.
  • Power rests on a single indicator.
  • Much of the backlog growth comes from a few large contracts; demand may lean on single customers.

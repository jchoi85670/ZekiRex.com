  • Never: leveraged funds, options trading, products promising 10%+ yields, chasing hot stocks

3. Life insurance: whose insurance is it?

Term insurance protects a family if the earner dies. A family with money does not need that. For a wealthy family, insurance is a tool to grow safe money tax-free, borrow against it and pass it on.

PolicyHow it growsFits
TermNone — pure coveragePaycheck earners whose family relies on their income
Whole life (top mutual insurer)Guaranteed cash value + dividends, about 3.5–5% a year long-runEstate-tax cash · bond-like base
IUL (indexed universal life)Index-linked with a cap and floor (e.g., 15% cap, 0.75% floor)Wealthy families growing safe money above bonds and borrowing against it
PPLI (private placement life)Index funds grow inside the policy tax-free, about 0.7%/yr cost$30M+ (qualified purchaser = $5M+ investments; $1–2M minimum premium)
What IUL can doWith an index fund
Death benefit from day one, income-tax freeNone
No loss in crash years (the floor)−37% in 2008
Policy loans: no credit check, no margin call, no repayment schedule, tax-free → a house or a businessSelling triggers tax; portfolio loans face margin calls in a crash
Grows tax-free; loans and death benefit tax-freeDividends taxed yearly, gains when sold

The risks are real too: insurers can cut caps (industry caps fell from about 12–13% in 2019 to 8–9% on many products in 2026), insurance charges rise with age, heavy loans can lapse the policy into a tax bill, and early surrender charges and the IRS funding limit (MEC) apply.

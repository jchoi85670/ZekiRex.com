Money from family in Korea: over $100,000 a year needs a US filing

ZekiRex™ US Sunday Edition · Sunday, November 8, 2026

This is the Sunday edition for families living in the United States. With the year-end approaching, many families receive a large sum from relatives in Korea. This week "Between Two Countries" works through the US reporting of money received from Korea, in numbers. The money itself carries no US tax, but a missed filing can carry a heavy penalty.

This Week's Person

Politics — the seventh Korean American elected to Congress, with roots in Jeju. In the November 3 election, Democrat Dan Koh, 41, won Massachusetts's 6th District. The AP called the race; with 77% of the vote counted he had 63%. His father is Korean American and his mother Lebanese American. His grandfather worked at the South Korean Embassy in Washington. Koh served as an aide to Boston's mayor and as a White House official, and he lost a 2018 primary in a different district. He is the seventh Korean American elected to the US Congress. This Week's Deep Dive — Between Two Countries

Money received from Korea: what to report in the US

The case (hypothetical). Ms. Kim, a green-card holder in California, received money twice in 2026 from her parents in Seoul. Her father sent $70,000 in March and her mother $50,000 in October. Neither parent is a US citizen or resident. We hold the exchange rate at the November 6 close of 1,356.6 won per dollar.

The short answer. She owes no US tax on the money. But because the year's total passed $100,000, she must file Form 3520. Gifts from both parents count together. (IRS: gifts from a foreign person)

1. What is due, and when

TaskDeadline
Form 3520, Part IV (money received in 2026)April 15, 2027; October 15 if you extend your income tax filing (Form 4868)
US citizens and residents living abroadJune 15, 2027
FBAR, if your Korean accounts together ever topped $10,000April 15, 2027, extended automatically to October 15 (IRS: FBAR)

Form 3520 is filed separately from the income tax filing. Once the total passes $100,000, each gift over $5,000 is listed on its own line. Ms. Kim lists both the March and October transfers.

2. Ms. Kim's numbers

The penalty is 5% of the gift for each month it goes unreported, capped at 25%. It does not apply if the delay had reasonable cause. In other words, money that carries no tax at all could cost $30,000.

3. If your situation differs

SituationRule
Each parent sends $60,000Gifts from related persons are combined: $120,000, so it must be reported
A "gift" from a Korean companyReport once 2026 gifts total over $20,573; the IRS may treat it as income
Parents pay tuition or medical bills directly to the school or hospitalNot treated as a foreign gift
The other way: Ms. Kim gives to family in Korea$19,000 per person is the 2026 annual exclusion; above that a gift tax filing may be needed (IRS: gift tax)

Remember three things. First, money received is a filing question, not a tax question. Second, the $100,000 count combines both parents. Third, gather the bank records with each transfer's date and amount now. Korea's own gift tax is decided separately, by where the recipient lives and where the property sits.

Talk to a professional before you act. Ask a US CPA about the US filings and a Korean tax accountant about Korean gift tax. This is general information based on IRS public material as of November 2026, not personal advice. The numbers leave out state taxes, Korean gift tax and exchange-rate moves. 2. Book now — Denali park buses for summer 2027

The interior of Alaska's Denali National Park is reached only by park bus. The park says summer bus tickets can be booked as early as December 1 of the year before. For a summer 2027 trip, book when the window opens on December 1, at reservedenali.com or 1-800-622-7275. In 2026 the bus to East Fork (Mile 43) costs $33.50 for adults; children 15 and under ride free but still need a ticket. The park entrance fee is a separate $15 per person for seven days. Citizens and permanent residents 62 and older can enter with a senior pass: $20 a year or $80 for life. (Denali buses, fees)

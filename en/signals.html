The evidence: 23 market indicators tested

Three findings

  1. What told us in advance was extreme fear. In the 13 weeks after a VIX weekly close of 25 or higher, the Nasdaq gained 7.4% on average (3.5% normally, 2006–2026) and Samsung + SK hynix 14.3% (8.8% normally, 2013–2026).
  2. No sell signal passed in both halves of history. Index trend, financial conditions, the yield curve, jobless claims and credit spreads each worked in only one period or cost too much return. The closest is the AI demand thermometer: both finished hot readings (2008, 2011) were followed by chip falls of more than 17%, but there was no finished case after 2015 to test the second half (hence WARNING).
  3. Margin debt came too early. US margin debt up 40% year over year switched on months before the 2000, 2007, 2021 and 2026 tops, but selling on it gave up too much return (failed). Results of trading on the AI thermometer are in the tables above.

When the fear signal fires — what, how much, how long

  • 20 fear episodes since 1998. A 13-week hold worked best; tech (XLK) had a median +10% and a worst episode of −21%.
  • Four turned into bear markets (2000–02, 2008, 2022) and lost money, so size by the worst drop you can sit through.
  • So what: fear was usually an opportunity, but this time it overlaps a hot AI demand thermometer, which makes it harder to read (panel ⑥).

What about leveraged ETFs?

  • Since 2010 alone, 3x looked brilliant; add 2000 and 2008 and 3x Nasdaq lost 71% and 3x chips 83% in a single episode.
  • Sized to the same worst-case loss, leverage earned about the same or less than the plain ETF.
  • So what: leverage multiplied risk more than it added return.

How to read it

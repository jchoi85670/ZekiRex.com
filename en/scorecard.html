Definitions — what “5 · 20 · 60 trading days” measures

The three numbers are not ranges. Each is a separate snapshot, all measured from the same starting point (D0).

D+5
D+20
D+60
▲ D0 close on the day it ran
D0 · base price
The close on the day the stock ran in the digest. For a weekend or holiday issue, the last close before it. Once set, it never changes.
D+5 · 5-day result
Return from the D0 close to the close on the 5th trading day after. Moves in between are ignored.
D+20 · 20-day result
D0 close → close on the 20th trading day. Cumulative, including the first 5 days — not “day 5 to day 20”.
D+60 · 60-day result
D0 close → close on the 60th trading day. About three months.
Excess vs. index
Minus the index return (KOSPI / S&P 500) between the same two dates, in percentage points. Above 0 = beat the index.
Trading day
A day the stock actually traded. Weekends and that market’s holidays don’t count. Korean stocks use the Korean calendar, U.S. stocks the U.S. one.
No result yet
Left blank until the D+N close exists. Never filled with 0.
E.g. HPE ran at the 9/14 (Mon) close of $55.41 → 5th trading day 9/21 (Mon) close $61.75 = +11.4%. S&P 500 over the same two dates +1.9% → +9.5%p vs. the index.

ZekiRex™ App User Guide

Last updated 2026-11-18

The ZekiRex™ app applies ZekiRex™'s own rules — strong stocks, at the right time, by a set standard — to about 2,400 US and 360 Korean stocks every day, and shows everyone the same result. This page explains ① what the app is for ② the thinking behind it ③ the evidence ④ how to read and use each screen.

We dissect your stocks and the market with fixed rules and show you what we find. You make the call.

1. What the app is for

🎯 Beat the market over timeThe yardsticks are the S&P 500 (VOO) and Nasdaq 100 (QQQM). If a method can't beat them, owning the index is better.
🛡️ Keep big losses smallA −50% loss needs +100% to recover. So the sell line is decided before the buy.
🧭 Rules, not feelingsNo "it might go higher" or "I hate to sell." The same numbers give everyone the same answer.
⏱️ A few minutes a dayEverything is recalculated automatically after each close. You review the result and decide.

2. Philosophy — how we think about it

ZekiRex™ builds on the big ideas of trend-and-breakout investing from William O'Neil (CAN SLIM) and Mark Minervini (SEPA). We did not copy the books: we tested every idea our own way and kept only what won. The exact thresholds and formulas are ZekiRex™ trade secrets and are not published.

① Look at what is already strongNot weak stocks that look cheap, but strong stocks the market has already chosen. The app's RS score (1–99) shows that strength.
② Rest, then break outA stock that has rested (built a base) and then breaks out is the signal. We don't chase breakouts that already happened.
③ Cut losers fast, let winners runSet the stop before buying; after a gain, hold to a set sell line. The app shows both.
④ Beware climaxesTops that rise too fast are flagged as a warning (climax top).
⑤ Rest when the market is weakWhen the whole market is in a downtrend, we wait.
⑥ Only what the data provesA rule that loses in testing stays out, however good it sounds.

3. Evidence — how we verify

Before adoption, every rule goes through repeated simulations and paired comparisons on long US and Korean price histories. The yardstick is always the S&P 500 (VOO) and Nasdaq 100 (QQQM) over the same period. Changes that don't beat them are dropped.

We weight the real record above backtests. The app's 🧪 Model Portfolio runs a virtual $100,000 on the same rules and publishes it daily since 2026-11-11 (−0.8% through 11/18 vs VOO −3.9% · QQQ −4.5%).

Limits. Tests on past data do not guarantee the future, and fees, taxes and fills are only partly modeled. That is why the Model Portfolio keeps checking the rules in real time.

6. My assets — add, edit, delete

💼 My assets is where you keep the stocks you own and see how each one is doing. Scans, charts and compare all work without it. The app never connects to a brokerage account and never places orders.

6-1. Add one stock

StepWhat to do
1Tap “＋ Buy · sell record” (or “＋ Add my stocks →” the first time).
2Choose an account. The first time, name it anything you like (for example “Brokerage A”). You can have several accounts.
3Type in the stock field and matches appear below. Search by ticker (F, AAPL) or by name. Tap a match to select it.
4Enter date, quantity and price per share, then Save. Buying the same stock again recalculates your average cost.
5For a sale, choose Sell at the top and enter it the same way. Realized profit is tracked separately.

6-2. Add many holdings at once

If you already own several stocks, tap “📥 Add many holdings at once” and paste one stock per line in the order stock, quantity, average cost, first buy date (the date is optional). You can also save your broker’s table as a CSV file and load it with “📄 Choose CSV file”.

You typeWhat goes in
AAPL, 10, 185.5, 2024-03-0210 shares of Apple at an average cost of 185.5, first bought 2024-03-02
MSFT, 5, 4105 shares of Microsoft at 410

Before saving you see how many stocks will be added and any lines that could not be read. If US and Korean stocks are mixed, they go into two accounts (adding dollars and won in one account would give a wrong total).

Tap an account row at the top and its stocks open right there, with current price, today’s change and return since buy. Tap a stock to open its chart.

6-5. Reading the holdings scorecard

All your holdings in one table. Darker colour means a bigger move. The default order is what to look at first (below the sell line → near it → furthest behind the index). Tap a column title to sort by it.

ColumnMeaning
Today · 1W · 1MThe stock’s own price change over that period
Since buyReturn versus your average cost
vs index (desktop)How far ahead of or behind the index (S&P 500 for US, KOSPI for Korea) since your first buy

The sell line is a line calculated by the rule, not a recommendation to sell. For stocks you did not buy with this rule (long-term holdings, for example), treat it as reference only.

10. Using it well — five minutes a day

#Habit
1Start with 🌤️ Market weather. In a "Market downtrend", watch — don't buy.
2Add ⭐ Stocks → Near buy names to your watchlist (☆). Look at the ones closest to their buy point first.
3The buy day is the breakout day only. Don't chase a breakout that already happened ("Hold").
4Set the stop before you buy — the app shows the price.
5＋ Log your trades so 💼 My assets can compare you with the indexes.
6Check 🔥 Money flow for leading sectors and look there first.
7Watch the 🧪 Model Portfolio to see whether the rules are working now. Rules can be wrong too.

11. FAQ

Q. Does "Buy" mean I should buy?
No. "Buy" is the output of fixed rules, not a recommendation. Decisions and their results are yours.

Q. The price differs slightly from my broker.
Intraday prices are about 15 minutes delayed. Buy-point decisions use the close and are recalculated after the market closes.

Q. What is the RS score?
A stock's price strength compared with other stocks, 1–99. Higher is stronger.

Q. Can I find Korean stocks in English?
Yes — type "Samsung" or "삼성"; both work.

Q. Who can see my records?
They are encrypted separately for each person and other users cannot see them. The operator does not open them. You can also keep them on your own device only, or delete everything at any time (6-6).

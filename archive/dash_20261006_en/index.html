One thing, in depth
Nasdaq at a high, and long-term yields still rising
On Oct. 5 the Nasdaq Composite rose to a 52-week high, with Nvidia's second straight record leading large technology names. Yet the 10-year yield rose again, on a day WTI fell below $90. Oil fell and long-term yields did not follow.
Why. Japan lifting its 10-year coupon to 3.1% points the same way. Higher Japanese yields give Japanese investors less reason to buy Treasuries, weakening one source of demand that has held US long-term yields down.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: whether the 10-year climbs above its Oct. 5 level, and whether WTI stays below $90.
For what we own: If you hold large technology stocks, note that the high came with the 10-year above 5%; if yields rise further, these are the first names to be repriced. If you hold refiners, cheaper crude lowers input costs. The Marathon Petroleum shares we hold rose 2.64% on Oct. 5.

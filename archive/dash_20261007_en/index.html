One thing, in depth
The S&P 500 at a record while small companies fall behind
On Oct. 6 the S&P 500 rose to its first record since August, with AMD's record leading large technology names. The 10-year yield eased slightly to 5.27%. Yet the Russell 2000 fell the same day: even with yields lower, small companies did not follow.
Why. More figures the same day showed money concentrating in AI: August imports hit a record on AI chips, and SpaceX is borrowing to buy Nvidia chips. The gains are gathered in one theme rather than spread across the market.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: whether the 10-year climbs back above 5.31% after the minutes, and whether the Russell 2000 loses more Compass conditions.
For what we own: If you hold small companies, note that the Russell 2000 reads red on our Compass. If you hold large technology stocks, note that the record rests on a narrow set of names: if AI expectations crack, the names that carried the index move together. The Dell shares we hold rose 3.93% on Oct. 6.

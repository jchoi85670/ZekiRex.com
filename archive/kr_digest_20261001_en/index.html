Theme 1 — $54bn for Alaska LNG, and the money that runs to steel pipe

According to Maeil Business (Oct 1), President Trump was set to announce a US investment plan that includes Korea putting $54 billion (about KRW 73 trillion) into Alaska LNG. Washington first asked for $67 billion. The project needs a 1,300-km gas pipeline.

Seoul listed a Texas gas-fired power plant as the first project under its $200 billion US investment fund, and filed Alaska LNG and eight reactors for later talks. If the announcement reorders that list, arguments over returns may grow. Pipe stocks rose together on the news on the 30th.

Theme 2 — Trading houses, as freight and commodity prices rise together

The Korea Economic Daily (Oct 1) noted that a drawn-out Middle East conflict has lifted energy commodity prices and shipping rates together. Trading houses turn both into profit. By consensus, POSCO International's operating profit this year is KRW 1,474.8 billion and LX International's KRW 471.1 billion.

The trading houses are adding production assets such as minerals and gas. POSCO International, which has a gas production base in the US, is also named as a possible beneficiary of the Alaska LNG investment. GS Global rose 5.96% on the 30th.

Theme 3 — Rare-earth magnets, a supply chain outside China

According to the Korea Economic Daily (Oct 1), LS Cable will spend KRW 150 billion on a rare-earth permanent-magnet plant in Gumi, North Gyeongsang - Korea's first for EV and robot magnets, after a plant in Virginia.

Permanent magnets go into EV motors, wind turbines and robot drives. China controls more than 80% of rare-earth mining, refining and manufacturing, so carmakers and defence firms are looking for other suppliers. The US is also putting billions into an allied supply chain.

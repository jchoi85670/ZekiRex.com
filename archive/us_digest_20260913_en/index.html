An AI cracked a hardest math problem, and America marked 25 years since 9/11

ZekiRex US Briefing · Sunday, 13 September 2026 · The week outside the economy
No newspaper is published on Sunday. So this digest sets prices aside and takes stock of the week. Saturday carried the last edition of the week, and everything below comes from it.
Two things landed in the same week. America reached the 25th anniversary of 9/11. In those same days an artificial intelligence solved a mathematical problem people had not been able to solve. A week of remembering the last 25 years sat next to a week of worrying about the next 25.
This Week, People
Memorial services were held in New York, in Pennsylvania and at the Pentagon. This year's roll of the dead was not confined to those killed in 2001. People who fell ill from the dust and smoke of that morning keep being added to it. The toll of this attack grows rather than settles.
The weekend pages revisited the restaurant that once sat on top of that building — Windows on the World, more than 100 floors up the North Tower. It was one of the best known dining rooms in New York and it changed how the city ate. A quarter of a century on, what survives is not the building but the stories of the people who worked inside it. (WSJ)
This Week, Society
Read the week through one number and the number is 4 million won. That is where monthly rents in the Guro and Dongdaemun districts of Seoul have arrived. Six months ago the same rent was a million won lower. What makes it news is that the figure came from outside Gangnam.
Gyeonggi province was not quiet either. In the districts built around semiconductor plants, jeonse deposits rose faster than in Seoul itself. Housing costs are following the jobs. A proposal for a dedicated guarantee-financing scheme for registered landlords, meant to hold rents down, ran the same day. (Hankyung)
This Week, Science and Technology
An artificial intelligence solved one of the Millennium problems, long counted among the hardest open questions in mathematics. The Wall Street Journal read the result as evidence that the pace of AI progress has stepped up.
A different scene appeared in Korea the same week. AI systems have begun handing work to other AI systems. They now generate their own next objective without a person setting the task. (Hankyung)
The unease stopped being a specialist concern. In offices and at dinner tables people talked about whether this technology could end civilisation. The worry was not purely abstract. Anthropic said it had found and shut down an Iranian attempt to use its own model to track US Navy warships. Look back on this week in five years and what lasts will not be the date a problem fell. It will be remembered as the week ordinary people began to be afraid of the technology.
This Week, Politics
Houthi fighters took an island and a coastal town on the Bab al-Mandeb strait, the entrance to the Red Sea. Saudi Arabia halted the pipeline it uses to bypass Hormuz after it came under attack — a line that moves up to 7 million barrels a day. Between them the two events pushed crude above $100. (WSJ)
Washington was running a different calculation. At the party convention in Dallas the president said every American would receive $5,000 if Republicans win the midterms. Objections about the deficit and about prices followed immediately. The promise was made days before the meeting that sets interest rates. (Hankyung)
US officials concluded that before the July strike which killed three American troops, Iran had obtained high-resolution satellite imagery from Chinese sources. The finding was made public ahead of the Trump-Xi meeting. That is the part investors should mark. If the meeting sours, tariffs and supply chains move first.

One thing, in depth
The 10-year yield hits a 19-year high and oil climbs too
In New York on Sept. 28 the 10-year Treasury yield rose to a 19-year high (Barron's). Oil climbed as US-Iran talks stalled, and yields rose with it. The S&P 500, the Nasdaq Composite and the Dow all fell.
Why. Oil is driving yields. Higher crude raises inflation worries, and the bond market reads that as room for further Fed hikes. Higher yields shrink what future profits are worth today, so technology shares and small companies, whose profits sit further out, feel it first.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: which way oil goes after the Iran talks, and Micron's Sept. 30 results.
For what we own: If you hold rate-sensitive names, watch oil first, because oil is setting the direction of yields. Growth stocks and small, indebted companies feel it first. Refiners and oil producers work the other way and are the first to wobble if a deal lowers oil. Twelve of the names clearing our standard are oil and gas companies.

→ Chip export controls stay as they are, so nothing changes for chipmakers with large China sales. Sellers of consumer electronics and farm goods benefit first.

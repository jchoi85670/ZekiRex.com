One thing, in depth
The Fed leans to a hike while long yields sit at the top of their range
On Oct. 7 the minutes tilted toward another hike, and the 10-year auction cleared at 5.300% the same day. The S&P 500 fell 0.22% the day after its record, while the Russell 2000 fell 1.31%. On a day yields rose, the smaller, more indebted companies fell first.
Why. The same day brought reports that AI chip buyers are borrowing to pay for them, which ties part of chip demand to interest rates. If long yields climb further, the interest on that debt grows.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: whether the 10-year holds above 5.3%, and how Brent at $104 shows up in refining stocks.
For what we own: If you hold small companies, note that the Russell 2000 reads red. If you hold AI-related stocks, watch the borrowing costs of the companies buying the chips: higher rates can shake demand and valuations together. The Micron shares we hold rose 4.06% on Oct. 7.

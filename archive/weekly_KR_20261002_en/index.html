The first week after the autumn holiday opened lower. The benchmark started September 28 at 6,889.74, 2.70% below its pre-holiday close, and fell for three sessions on foreign selling. Micron's results and a record September export figure turned it. It rose 1.95% on October 1 and closed October 2 at 7,003.74, up 1.65% on the week. Our simulated portfolio gained 0.29% over the same period and trailed the index.
The five things that mattered this week
The five events of the week that touched money. Each one runs: what happened, why, and what it means.
1
Seoul fell for three days after the holiday, then regained 7,000 in two
FACT  On September 30 the benchmark stood at 6,838.04, 3.43% below its September 23 pre-holiday close. Two rising sessions then took it back above 7,000.
WHY  Foreign investors sold 3.08 trillion won on September 29 alone, and on September 30 foreigners and institutions together sold 3 trillion won.
ACTION — The index is still below its 7,080.92 pre-holiday close even after absorbing the news that piled up abroad. Before treating the rebound as a trend, check whether foreign selling actually turns.
2
The American ten-year yield hit a 24-year intraday high
FACT  The American ten-year yield passed 5.33% intraday on October 1 (local time), its highest since 2002, before settling at 5.24%.
WHY  August personal consumption inflation came in below forecasts at 3.4%, yet yields still rose. Federal Reserve Governor Michael Barr said a further increase would likely be needed.
ACTION — When yields rise even as inflation cools, growth names valued on distant profits are marked down first. If you hold heavily borrowed companies, check how much interest cost takes out of this quarter's earnings.
3
September exports hit a record, with chips above $60 billion for the first time
FACT  The trade ministry said on October 1 that September exports reached a monthly record of about $120.9 billion, with semiconductors above $60 billion for the first time.
WHY  Exports for January to September total $814.5 billion, and analysts said an annual $1 trillion is within reach. (Dong-A Ilbo, Edaily)
ACTION — Exports this strong also lift the won. If you hold exporters, set the currency's drag against next quarter's earnings when you look at them.
4
Micron's results pulled Seoul's chip stocks higher
FACT  Micron reported fourth-quarter revenue of about $54.2 billion on September 30 (local time) and guided next quarter to $61.5 billion.
WHY  Semiconductors and equipment rose 11.57% on the week, and on October 1 foreigners and institutions bought close to 900 billion won on the junior market.
ACTION — A whole industry rose on one company's numbers. If your account leans to chips, allow for the same names falling together for the same reason before the next reports.
5
Financials went the other way
FACT  By industry the week showed banks down 3.51%, life insurance 3.38% and property insurance 2.92%.
WHY  The rising side was electronic equipment up 15.85%, chips, and electrical products 9.72%. The groups that lifted the index and the financials moved in opposite directions.
ACTION — The week ran against the view that high rates favour banks. If you hold banks or insurers, watch first for when buying concentrated in chips turns, rather than the level of rates.

The five things to know for next week
Five that follow from this week, each with what to watch.
1
Only three trading days in Seoul
FACT  The exchange is shut on October 5 for the National Foundation Day substitute holiday and on October 9 for Hangul Day. Trading runs October 6 to 8.
WHY  New York trades on both days, so news from abroad on those dates lands all at once at the next Seoul open.
ACTION — Anything bought on October 8 cannot be touched for three days. Before adding, work out whether you can carry the moves that arrive during the break.
2
Micron's Taiwan union vote closes October 6
FACT  The union at Micron's Taoyuan plant in Taiwan is holding a strike vote over profit sharing that closes on October 6.
WHY  The union cited the bonus schemes at Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.
ACTION — A strike would tighten memory supply, which supports Korean memory names, but check how much of that hope is already in their prices.
3
OPEC+ meets on October 4
FACT  OPEC+ is set to keep output targets unchanged at its Sunday, October 4 meeting, Reuters reported.
WHY  West Texas Intermediate fell 1.45% this week to $91.3, while Brent climbed back above $100 on October 1. (CNBC)
ACTION — Crude is one of the forces lifting American long yields. Without an output increase that pressure stays, so for airlines and chemicals, where fuel is a large cost, wait for the outcome before deciding.
4
AMD chief Lisa Su visits Seoul on October 8
FACT  AMD chief Lisa Su meets Korean AI chip designers including Rebellions and DeepX in Seoul on October 8. (Maeil Business)
WHY  It is her first visit in seven months and falls on Seoul's last trading day of the week.
ACTION — Any announcement leaves a single session to react before the holiday. If you buy ahead on design-house hopes, allow for the closed day that follows.
5
Hyundai Marine & Fire: 0.96% above its stop
FACT  Hyundai Marine & Fire closed October 2 at 47,650 won against a stop at 47,196, leaving 0.96% of room.
WHY  Property insurance fell this week, and when a group falls together a single name rarely holds up alone.
ACTION — We do not sell early because room has narrowed; we sell the whole position at the close that ends below the set price. If you hold the same group, decide your own exit now.

What You Have To Know Today
In Seoul too the index held while everything under it gave way
In the 1 September Asian session the KOSPI closed 0.23% higher at 6,835.80 while the KOSDAQ fell 1.56% to 821.25 and the Nikkei slipped 0.15%. It repeats the previous Seoul session, where retail, institutions and foreign investors all sold and only other corporates bought.
Why. The same narrowing is showing on both sides of the Pacific. Asia split index from small caps; New York split megacaps from the rest. One phenomenon under two names, with one cause: as rates rise the discount rate rises, and whatever is most sensitive to it gives way first.
What follows. Friday's US jobs report is the week's axis. Consensus is +55,000 nonfarm payrolls and a 4.2% unemployment rate, but July was a decline of 23,000. The estimate assumes a rebound; if it misses, the rate path moves again. Before it: JOLTS and ISM manufacturing Tuesday, ADP and the Beige Book Wednesday.

An index held up by companies buying their own shares has a thick floor, because unlike investors who sell on price this buyer does not tire. So the thing to watch today is not the index level but whether institutions or foreign investors turn net buyers. Until that handover, the index stands on a finite resource.

What You Have To Know Today
Behind a 0.5% index day, the leaders were cut 6%
In New York on September 14 the S&P 500 fell 0.48% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.56%. It looks like a quiet session. In the same hours the 10-year Treasury yield rose as far as 5.011%, its highest since October 2023, and came back to close below the 5% line. That line was touched and given up inside one day. The loss did not spread evenly. The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell close to 6%, with Micron down 5.25% and AMD down 4.4%, while refiners barely moved: Marathon Petroleum rose 0.13% and HF Sinclair fell 0.86%.
Why. Two presses count the same day differently. Korean outlets led with the fear of tightening out of the United States and put the slump in shipbuilders and power-equipment makers in their headlines. US outlets named the shut Saudi pipeline and the crude price as the cause of the move in rates. The split is over whether to show the effect or the cause first. Our own numbers point at a third place. When the yield reaches the 5% line, the discount rate that converts distant profit into a price today rises with it. A business earning a cash margin now is left alone; a business whose profit arrives in several years is marked down with no news from the company. The industry ranking moved in exactly that shape: power equipment and engineering and construction lost the most places, and semiconductors and electronic components lost ground too.
What follows. The answer arrives at this week's Federal Reserve meeting. The question is whether a price rise that began in supply can be treated with the policy rate at all. Morgan Stanley has followed Goldman Sachs into a hike call and market-implied odds are above 92%. So what to watch is not the decision but whether the 10-year settles above 5%. If it stays in the 4.9s after the decision, Monday's retreat continues; if it hardens above 5%, the leadership is cut again.
For what we own: If you hold semiconductors or AI equipment, the number to re-examine this week is the discount rate rather than the revenue forecast. Once the 10-year settles above 5%, the same forecast is worth less, with no announcement from the company. The other side belongs here too. If you hold refiners, it is today's refining margin holding the price up, which is why they did not move on Monday. Yet the same level of rates eventually reaches the demand for the product, and a hike landing on top of a supply shock shortens that road. One event settles the question: the Federal Reserve decision this week. Raising rates reduces demand; it does not reopen a blocked pipeline.

→ Once the deal closes the counterparty on power contracts changes, so for the largest buyers of power the name on the contract moves before the price does.

→ A lower food price level muddies how the Bank of Japan reads its own price data, so the yen and long-dated Japanese bonds may move before anything else does.
Korea and the US: The shock reaches New York twice. It arrives first as rates and the crude price. It arrives again through the manufacturing costs of Asian plants, inside the memory and components American buyers purchase. In Seoul on September 15 the KOSPI closed a shade lower, with Samsung Electronics down 0.2% and SK Hynix down 0.41%, while the won weakened 12.1 to touch 1,360 per dollar. A softer won lifts part of the cost burden off Korean exporters and holds down the component prices American buyers pay.

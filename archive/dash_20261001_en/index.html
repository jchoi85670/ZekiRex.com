One thing, in depth
Yields rise on cooler inflation as growth takes the wheel
PCE inflation came in below forecasts on Sept. 30, yet the 10-year yield rose, meaning growth rather than inflation is pulling rates. Micron then beat after the close, and Seoul and Tokyo bought first on Oct. 1.
Why. When inflation drives rates, cooler inflation data brings yields down. Now yields rose on cooler data, so good economic news adds to the rate burden, and even strong chip earnings can be marked down against yields.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: whether the regular New York session on Oct. 1 follows Micron higher, and whether the 10-year climbs further.
For what we own: If you hold chipmakers, watch the 10-year alongside Micron's numbers; strong earnings will struggle to lift shares if yields keep climbing. Refiners work the other way, since fewer Chinese fuel exports lift product prices. Four of the 64 names clearing our standard are refiners.

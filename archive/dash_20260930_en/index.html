One thing, in depth
Long Treasury yields climb even as oil falls
In New York on Sept. 29 the 30-year Treasury yield rose further (Wall Street Journal). Oil fell the same day as Gulf exports recovered. Stocks pared losses after Williams spoke, but long-term yields did not come down.
Why. Until the last edition we read oil as the driver of yields. On Sept. 29 long yields rose even as oil fell. That means buyers of long bonds are worried about inflation and government debt together, so long yields may not come down quickly even if oil calms.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: whether the 30-year climbs further, and Micron's Sept. 30 results.
For what we own: If you hold growth stocks, watch the 30-year before oil; the further out a company's profits sit, the more sensitive it is to long yields. Refiners and oil producers work the other way and wobble first when oil falls. Of the names clearing our standard, 12 are oil and gas companies and 5 are chipmakers.

→ Canadian Natural (CNQ) and Cenovus Energy (CVE) on our list sit 9.0% and 8.7% below their 52-week highs. The wider export outlet is years away, so it does not reach earnings soon.
Oura postpones its $15 billion listing · The BBC and the Guardian reported on Sept. 29 that smart ring maker Oura postponed its listing, citing market uncertainty. Bloomberg wrote that some investors pushed back on its valuation. (BBC Business · Guardian Business · Bloomberg)
→ CNBC reported that IPO postponements are accelerating in the third quarter, meaning new shares are priced lower while long yields rise. If you plan to buy into an upcoming listing, watch whether offer prices are coming down.
Trump's new tariffs go back to court on Sept. 30 · The New York Times reported that on Sept. 30 the Trump administration goes to court again to defend its new tariffs. G20 trade ministers open two days of talks in Milwaukee the same day (France 24). (NYT Business · France 24)
→ No ruling date has been set. If the courts block the tariffs, import prices fall and inflation pressure eases, one of the few things that could hold down long yields now rising.
French inflation hits 3.4%, the highest in more than two years · Bloomberg reported on Sept. 30 that France's statistics agency put September inflation at 3.4%, up from 2.6% in August, driven by oil and natural gas costs. (Bloomberg)
→ Bloomberg said the reading keeps pressure on the European Central Bank to keep raising rates. If Europe is still hiking, US long yields are unlikely to come down on their own. If you hold bonds, watch the direction of European rates too.
Korea and the US: Seoul took the long-term Treasury yield through foreign and institutional selling, while Tokyo bought the Micron hopes first. President Trump is set to announce Korea's US investment at 15:30 ET on Sept. 30 (Maeil Business), reportedly including $54 billion for Alaska gas.

Why — stocks found in the news
Theme 1 — US yields at a 19-year high, and Korea's banks

The US 5-year Treasury yield touched 5.03% intraday on the 23rd, above 5% for the first time since 2007. A $70 billion 5-year auction drew weak demand, and its 5.033% yield was the highest since June 2006 (Maeil Business, Sep 25).

Markets expect the Fed to raise rates again; the chance of a 0.25-point hike next month is 69% (IBD, Sep 24).

When rates stay high for longer, the interest banks earn on loans stays high too. That is why the research heads named banks as a high-rate beneficiary. Yoo Jong-woo of Korea Investment & Securities advised keeping weightings in chips, banks and insurers (Maeil Business, Sep 25).

KB, Shinhan, Hana and Woori all have trend gauges above zero and rose 14.7% to 21.4% over three months. IBK's trend gauge is below zero, so we left it out.

Theme 2 — Life insurers, whose invested money earns more when rates rise

Insurers put the premiums they collect into bonds. When new bonds pay more, investment income rises. The research heads advised keeping insurers alongside banks (Maeil Business, Sep 25).

Among life insurers, institutions bought a net 3.75 million shares of Hanwha Life over 20 sessions, placing it among the top five institutional buys in the names we follow.

Theme 3 — Theme set aside today — Power equipment

The research heads listed power equipment as a winner from data-centre power shortages (Maeil Business, Sep 25). But HD Hyundai Electric is down 23.9% over three months with a Zeki Strength of 27. LS Electric has a Zeki Strength of 91, but its trend gauge is still below zero, as is Hyosung Heavy Industries'. We want to see the trend turn first.

One more set aside for the same reason — Kia. In Mexico, President Lee Jae-myung proposed restarting free-trade talks, citing Kia's electric-vehicle plant in Nuevo Leon as an example of cooperation. Since this year Mexico has charged 10% to 50% tariffs on some 1,400 items, cars included, from countries without a trade deal (Maeil Business, Sep 25). But Kia is down 11.6% over 20 sessions, its Zeki Strength is 26 and its trend gauge is below zero.

What it is. A record of the companies we found in the newspapers and named in the digest, marked at that day's close, then counted as the price moves. Grouped by how firm the evidence was.
What it is for. To keep a number on whether names picked from the news actually perform. Each name is recorded on the day it ran, so the winners cannot be chosen afterwards.
What it is not. Not a recommendation, not something we bought, and not yet a verdict. That comes once, from a pre-registered test, when both markets together reach 60 cases.
How long a record. Most of these numbers cover a few days. Prices rise for a few days and fall for a few days; the shorter the window, the more flattering — or the more punishing — it looks.

One thing, in depth
The 10-year hits 5.33% and turns; the 2-year falls to 4.61%
On Oct. 1 the 10-year broke its highest since 2002 intraday and closed at 5.24%. The 2-year fell to 4.61%, meaning the Fed remarks erased near-term hike bets first. The same day WTI rose 2.71% and the dollar index climbed to 102.1; oil and the dollar did not follow yields down.
Why. The 2-year prices the Fed's next few months; the 10-year prices growth and inflation further out. The Fed remarks erased the near-term hike bets but left the longer end alone. While oil keeps rising, the case for the 10-year falling below 5% is still thin.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: whether the 10-year falls further below 5.24% after the Oct. 2 jobs report, and whether Europe decides to release diesel reserves.
For what we own: If you hold rate-sensitive growth stocks, it is too early to read the Oct. 1 reversal as a turn. A weak jobs report on Oct. 2 would extend it; a strong one could send the 10-year back to test 5.33%. Refiners rose with oil, but the risk cuts the other way too: a European diesel release would squeeze refining margins, and the four refiners among the 61 names clearing our standard would move together.

ON Semiconductor buys Synaptics for $123 a share in cash, in a smaller deal · ON Semiconductor agreed on Oct. 1 to buy Synaptics for $123 a share, about $5.7 billion, in cash rather than stock. The deal is smaller than first planned (Wall Street Journal). (Wall Street Journal)
→ The buyer pays cash rather than stock with the 10-year above 5%. If you hold a chipmaker with a pending acquisition, check first how much the financing cost takes out of its profit.
Amazon seeks to offload $8 billion of Nvidia chips to investors · The Financial Times reported on Oct. 2 that Amazon is seeking to offload $8 billion of Nvidia chips to investors, to ease the balance-sheet strain of its AI spending. (Financial Times · Reuters)
→ Buyers of AI hardware are starting to move the cost off their books. If you hold server or storage makers, separate whether customers are cutting spending or only changing how they fund it.
The US Navy awards Raytheon a $24.4 billion Standard Missile-6 contract · Reuters reported on Oct. 1 that the US Navy awarded RTX's Raytheon a $24.4 billion contract for Standard Missile-6, amid concerns over depleted stockpiles. (Reuters · The Straits Times)
→ A contract of this size turns into revenue over several years. If you hold defense stocks, watch whether production capacity keeps up with delivery schedules before the headline amount.
New Mexico seeks up to $40 billion in penalties from Meta; the ruling is still ahead · Reuters reported on Oct. 1 that New Mexico is seeking up to $40 billion in penalties from Meta after a data privacy trial. The court has yet to rule. (Reuters · Channel NewsAsia)
→ The amount sought is not a ruling. If you hold advertising stocks, watch whether the decision restricts how ads are targeted before you weigh the dollar figure.
Twilio joins the S&P 500 on Oct. 6, replacing Warner Bros. Discovery · Twilio enters the S&P 500 before the open on Oct. 6, taking the place of Warner Bros. Discovery, which is merging with Paramount (Investor's Business Daily, MarketWatch). (Investor's Business Daily · MarketWatch)
→ Index inclusion brings mechanical buying from index funds on the day. If you buy on the news, bear in mind that this demand ends on the inclusion date.
Korea and the US: In Seoul on Oct. 2 (local time) the KOSPI rose 0.46% to 7,003.74, back above 7,000 for the first time in five sessions (Money Today). Foreign investors sold more than 20 trillion won of Korean stocks in September (Maeil Business).

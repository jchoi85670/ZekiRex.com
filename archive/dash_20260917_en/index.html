What You Have To Know Today
The first increase in three years did not strike the whole market
The Federal Reserve raised its policy rate on Sept. 16. The Dow fell 1.21% that session while the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.01%. The 10-year rose to 5.01% and the 2-year to 4.40%, and the dollar index rose to 100.31. As yields rose, the side that borrows to operate was cut first: capital markets fell 28 places to 76th. Crude fell 3.21% the same day.
Why. Two presses count the same decision differently. US outlets led with a press conference read as hawkish and with the fall in the indexes. Korean outlets wrote that the worry was already in the price. The gap showed in the currency rather than in equities: at the Seoul close on Sept. 17 the Kospi ended 2.56 points lower at 6,715.41, and the Korean currency fell 13.6 to ₩1,382.2 per dollar. (Reuters, Money Today, Edaily) What is at issue is less the decision than what follows it, and Goldman Sachs moved its next call to October.
What follows. Watch the Bank of Japan decision on Sept. 18 alongside European gas storage: Reuters reported that low European inventories could spike liquefied natural gas prices this winter.
For what we own: If you hold banks, brokers or property — anything that borrows to operate — this increase may not be the last. Sixteen of the eighteen projections see another this year, so the line to check is interest expense rather than the price target. If you hold refiners, look somewhere else: crude fell on Sept. 16 and refining rose, because refining profit comes from the gap between crude and product rather than from the crude price itself. Check the refining margin before the oil headline. That two of our four positions are refiners is also the weakness of this reading: if the margin narrows, both fall together.

Where money leaving Asia goes next is what changes US bond demand in the week ahead.

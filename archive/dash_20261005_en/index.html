One thing, in depth
Weak jobs lift stocks, but long-term yields rise
After the weak jobs report on Oct. 2 the Nasdaq Composite climbed to just below its 52-week high. Stocks read it as the Fed no longer needing to raise rates. Yet 2-year and 10-year yields both rose, on a day WTI fell 1.9%. Yields rose even as oil fell.
Why. Yields not following oil down means inflation fear alone is not lifting them. Bloomberg wrote that high oil and surging yields have blocked the rally from broadening into more sectors. The Russell 2000 staying red fits the same pattern.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: whether the 10-year climbs above its Oct. 2 level, and whether WTI drops below $90 after the Saudi price cut.
For what we own: If you hold large technology stocks, note that the run to near-record levels came with the 10-year above 5%. If yields rise further, these are the first names to be repriced. If this week's Fed minutes confirm the end of hikes, that pressure eases. The Micron shares we bought on Oct. 2 sit inside the same risk.

What You Have To Know Today
Jobs beat forecasts threefold, and rate-cut hopes fade
US August payrolls came in far above forecasts. The Labor Department said the economy added 162,000 jobs, against the 55,000 the market expected, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1% instead of rising to 4.2%. After July's unexpected loss of 23,000, the direction has turned completely.
Why. The good news pushed prices down. Just after the open the Dow was off 0.4% and the S&P 500 0.1%, with only the Nasdaq slightly higher. A strong labour market gives the Federal Reserve less reason to cut quickly, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose back to 4.77%.
What follows. What to watch next is the Federal Reserve meeting on 15-16 September. The inflation data before it will set the direction for rates, and that direction passes straight through to the growth names now under pressure.
For what we own: What to watch is the Federal Reserve meeting on 15-16 September. Until yesterday the expectation leaned toward holding; today's jobs number could unsettle that.

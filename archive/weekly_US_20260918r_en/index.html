The week in one sentence. Read the index and it rose; count the stocks and it fell. The Standard and Poor's 500 added 0.40% and the Nasdaq composite 1.28%. Over the same five sessions the Dow lost 1.41% and the Russell 2000 lost 1.10%. Of the 1,039 US names we follow, the 1,033 that traded every session averaged a 1.25% decline. One group lifted the index: semiconductors, 27 names, up 8.35%, and the largest of all groups by market value. Whatever sells computing equipment or the power to run it rose together; whatever sells to consumers or bills by the hour fell together. The line between the two was the 10-year US government bond yield, which finished at 5.00%.
The five things that mattered this week
The five events of the week that touched money. Each one runs: what happened, why, and what it means.
1
The index and the stocks finished in opposite directions
FACT  The Standard and Poor's 500 rose 0.40%. The 1,033 names that traded all five sessions averaged a 1.25% decline. The Dow fell 1.41% and the Russell 2000 fell 1.10%.
WHY  An index weighs its members by market value. When the 27 semiconductor names, the heaviest group of all, rise 8.35%, the index can rise while most of its members fall. Of 105 industry groups, only 24 finished higher.
ACTION — Measuring your own account against the index will mislead you this week. If what you hold sits outside semiconductors and power equipment, the week the index describes is not the week your account had. What you owned mattered more than which way the market went.
2
Refiners rose while crude fell
FACT  Oil and gas refining and marketing rose 7.44%, second among all groups. Oil and gas exploration and production fell 4.73%. West Texas Intermediate slipped 1.08% to finish at $100.3.
WHY  What the market paid for was the refining margin, not the barrel. A refiner buys crude and sells product, so a lower barrel widens the spread it earns. For a producer the same fall is simply less revenue.
ACTION — Treating energy as one block hides what happened: refiners and producers finished 12.17 percentage points apart. If you carry an energy weighting, check which side of that split it actually sits on.
3
Anything tied to the consumer or to credit was sold all week
FACT  Auto and truck dealerships fell 9.95%, last of all 105 groups. Gambling lost 8.50%, restaurants 7.09% and advertising agencies 6.80%.
WHY  The 10-year yield finished at 5.00%. For a dealer that finances its sales, the rate is the demand; restaurants and advertising are what households cut first. AutoNation and Lithia Motors both sit among the week's ten worst.
ACTION — If you hold names levered to consumer spending or borrowing, Costco's results on September 24 will confirm this reading or overturn it. It is the earliest clean look at what US households are actually doing.
4
Seven of the week's ten best were chips, optics or power
FACT  Semtech rose 25.81%, Vicor 20.57%, Coherent 19.08%, Astera Labs 17.98%, Generac 17.21%, Credo 17.19% and Sandisk 15.45%.
WHY  Electrical equipment rose 7.18% and semiconductor equipment 6.97%. What goes into building a data centre rose as one line. On the 17th Nvidia's chief executive said the company will sell twice as many chips next year as this year. (CNBC)
ACTION — This group rises on next year's build plans, not on this quarter's results. It moved on remarks rather than orders, so if you hold the theme, the thing to verify is whether those capital plans turn into booked orders in the quarterly numbers.
5
Our four names sat on both sides of the split
FACT  Marathon Petroleum rose 7.17%, HF Sinclair 8.41%, Dell Technologies 6.32% and Iridium 1.28%. The book finished the week up 3.62%.
WHY  Three of the four sit inside this week's six strongest groups: two refiners and one computer hardware name. Only Iridium sits in telecom services, which fell 4.65%, and Iridium is the only one of the four below its purchase price.
ACTION — If that arrangement turns, the two refiners fall together. This week's result says the positions set back in August happened to match this week's market, not that the week was well traded. In a week that does not match, the same structure costs the same amount.

The five things to know for next week
Five that follow from this week, each with what to watch.
1
September 24, Costco: whether the consumer really cracked
FACT  Costco reports on September 24, with the market looking for 11.6% earnings growth. TD Synnex reports the same day, where the expected figure is 30.7%.
WHY  The groups that fell hardest this week were car retailing and restaurants, so prices already assume household spending is slowing. Costco is the earliest number that tests whether that assumption is right.
ACTION — This is the day that decides whether the week's decline in consumer names was justified. Strong numbers make it a rate story; weak numbers make it a demand story, and the two call for different responses over the following weeks.
2
The Xi-Trump summit, with no date yet announced
FACT  President Xi Jinping of China travels to Washington next week. On September 20 in New York, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Vice Premier He Lifeng work through the detail. (South China Morning Post, Financial Times)
WHY  The groups that rose this week were semiconductors and semiconductor equipment, and both sit directly under export controls and tariffs. What the summit produces reaches next year's capital plans for exactly that cluster.
ACTION — If you hold chips or chip equipment, this meeting matters more than any earnings date in the window. Adding heavily before there is an outcome is a bet on one diplomatic calendar entry.
3
The 10-year at 5.00%, which is where next week opens
FACT  The 10-year US government bond yield rose 0.04 percentage points this week to finish at 5.00%, and is up 0.81 points since the start of the year.
WHY  This yield is what separated the two halves of the week. Car retailing, which finances its sales, fell 9.95%, while the cash-rich equipment names rose. If the yield stays here, the same separation continues.
ACTION — If you hold leveraged names, this week's fall may not be a passing dip. That structure needs the yield to come down before it recovers, so the rate path deserves your attention before the company's own results.
4
Whether the refining spread holds its width
FACT  Refining and marketing rose 7.44% while exploration and production fell 4.73%. West Texas Intermediate closed down 1.08% at $100.3.
WHY  Refiners rose this week because a falling barrel widened the spread they earn. If crude turns back up, the same mechanism reverses and refiners give way first.
ACTION — Two of our four holdings are refiners. If you are watching an energy weighting, following the gap between crude and product prices reads this stretch more accurately than following the barrel itself.
5
Whether the capex talk becomes orders: Micron on September 30
FACT  Micron reports on September 30. Among the 64 names that cleared our standard it carries the highest ZekiRex Strength reading at 98. A possible strike is being discussed at its main production base in Taiwan. (Edaily)
WHY  Chips and optics rose this week on remarks about next year's plans, not on orders. Micron's numbers are the first check on whether those plans have reached revenue. A strike would cut supply, which pushes prices the other way.
ACTION — The date falls beyond next week. Adding to the theme before it arrives means paying now for demand that has not been confirmed. If you hold memory names, first ask whether the size you carry can absorb the move around that day.

Next week earnings cross-examine the line this week drew. The market has already priced two separate economies: what capital spending buys went up, what households buy went down. The evidence on the winning side is still remarks and plans, while the evidence on the losing side is a 5.00% yield. On September 24 Costco and TD Synnex each attach a number to one of the two. If the consumer is not as weak as assumed, the groups that fell this week have room to come back first; if technology shipments disappoint, the groups that rose are the ones that correct. Between those, the US-China talks could change the terms for anything semiconductor-related. With all four seats filled we will not be buying next week. Only the selling rules keep running.

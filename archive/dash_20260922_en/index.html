One thing, in depth
Crude fell 4.5% and technology and refining split apart
West Texas Intermediate fell 4.51% on Sept. 21 to $95.8 a barrel while the 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.96% and the 30-year to 5.30%. The dollar index was all but unchanged at 100.4, up 0.21%. With the currency still, crude and long rates fell together. The Nasdaq Composite rose 2.26% to 27,122, a record, and Advanced Micro Devices crossed a trillion dollars in market value. The same session took the four refining and marketing names down between 4.17% and 5.70%.
Why. The Korean and American desks count the same decline differently. American pages wrote that cheaper crude opened the rally. Korean pages led on rate and oil anxiety easing and pointed to foreign buying concentrated in semiconductors. The two accounts do not collide: one is about the discount rate and the other about who is buying. (The Wall Street Journal, Barron's, Investor's Business Daily, MK Markets)
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: the gap between crude and product, and the Washington meeting on Sept. 24. If the gap widens we change today's demand-side reading. Tariffs and rare earths sit on the same table at that meeting, so a cost outlook can move in a single day.
For what we own: If you hold refiners, the line to watch today is not the oil headline but the gap between crude and product, because that gap is where refining profit comes from. Sept. 21 came down on the side of the gap not widening. The other side belongs here too: if crude fell for supply reasons the gap widens again, and today's fall will have made those shares cheaper. If you hold technology, note that what lifted it was the rate rather than earnings, and a rate that rises again can take back the same amount.

→ Rare earths go into magnets and motors, so they reach the cost line of carmakers and defense suppliers, and that cost can be settled in one meeting. Adding heavily before the talks is a bet on a single room's outcome.
The EU fails to agree on extending Russia sanctions · The European Union did not agree to extend its Russia sanctions, which expire today, and talks on removing certain individuals from the list resume. (Euronews · France 24)
→ If sanctions loosen, Russian crude has a wider path to market, which puts this on the same axis as today's lead. If you hold energy, read it as a story about supply increasing.
Canada's central bank chief says tariffs could halve fourth-quarter growth · The governor of the Bank of Canada said fourth-quarter growth could be halved if US tariffs stay in place, and added that business investment may be put off. (Globe and Mail)
→ A combination that lifts prices while cutting growth narrows what a central bank can do. Our screen does not measure tariffs, so the list does not change. If you hold companies with large Canadian revenue, it will show up in the fourth-quarter figures.
India's exchange draws more than $10 billion of orders · India's National Stock Exchange drew more than $10 billion of orders for a $2.3 billion offering, the country's largest of the year. (CNBC)
→ An exchange fetching a high price means large expectations attached to fee income, and the capital-markets names among the US stocks we follow sit inside the same re-rating. If you hold brokers or exchanges, watch whether traded value is actually rising.
Korea and the US: What this Asian close gives a New York reader is confirmation of direction in rates. In Seoul on Sept. 22 the three-year government yield eased to 4.04%, the same direction long yields took in New York. When rates fall in both places at once it is the price of money moving rather than one country's affairs.

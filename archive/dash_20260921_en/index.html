One thing, in depth
Crude and long rates moved opposite ways on the same day
West Texas Intermediate fell 1.58% on Sept. 18 to $100.3 a barrel while the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.00%. The dollar index was unchanged at 100.2, so with no currency explanation what is left is bond supply. The 2-year barely moved at 4.42% and only the long end rose. Copper rose to $6.61, so there is still no signal that growth has rolled over.
Why. The Korean and American presses count the same weekend differently. The American desks led on falling crude as the reason stocks rose. The Korean desks led on Samsung Electronics' exports and dividend expectations. The two do not conflict: cheaper crude is a lower input cost, and that gain reaches the makers of things first.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: the refining margin and the Washington meeting on Sept. 24. If the gap narrows, the refining reading changes. Tariffs and crude sit on one table at that meeting, so the cost outlook can move in a single day.
For what we own: If you hold refiners, the line to watch is the refining margin rather than the oil headline. Refining profit comes from the gap between crude and product, and Sept. 18 came down on the side of that gap not having narrowed. The other side belongs here too: if crude is falling because demand is weakening, crude and product fall together and refining profit shrinks with them. Two of the four names we hold are refiners. Two names in one industry move the same way on the same day, so if the gap narrows they are shaken together.

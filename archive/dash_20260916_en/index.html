What You Have To Know Today
On a day all four indexes fell, four refiners rose
In New York on Sept. 15 the S&P 500 fell 0.45% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.78%, with the Dow down 0.63% and the Russell 2000 down 0.76%. The same session took the 10-year Treasury yield to 5.00% and lifted WTI 4.38%. What paid and what was paid came apart. Capital markets fell fifteen places from 29th in our industry ranking and gold from 5th to 10th, while the four refiners carry a ZekiRex Strength between 94 and 96.
Why. Two presses count the same day differently. US outlets led with retail sales up 1.2% and import prices up 0.7% as the case for an increase. Korean outlets weighed the same figures toward what Chair Warsh says at the press conference rather than the decision itself. Ed Yardeni, a bull, cut his S&P 500 target to 7,900 and named Treasury yields as the reason. The three point one way: what moves this phase is the discount rate, not earnings. (AP, WSJ, Edaily) One correction: the Sept. 15 edition wrote that yields had split the leadership. Semiconductors rose from 18th to 16th on the Sept. 15 close, so that reading does not stand. What yields cut was not the leadership but the side that pays interest.
What follows. The answer arrives with the Federal Reserve decision on Sept. 16 (local time). The question is whether a price rise that began in supply can be handled with a policy rate. An increase is already in the price; what moves the price is the wording at the press conference.
For what we own: If you hold refiners or energy, the number to re-examine this week is refining margin rather than the crude price. Refining profit comes from the gap between product and crude, and phases exist where crude rises while that gap narrows. If instead you hold long-dated bonds or growth names, a 10-year at 5.00% prices the same forecast at a lower value, and the price falls without the company announcing anything. That two of our four positions are refiners is also the weakness of this reading: if crude retraces, the two fall together.

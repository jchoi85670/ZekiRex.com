Wait — the stance the signal implies.

In New York on September 30 the S&P 500 fell 0.25% to 7,651.54. The Nasdaq Composite rose 0.24%, while the Dow lost 0.86% and the Russell 2000 0.39%. The 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.31% during the day, its highest since 2002. Alphabet, Apple, Amazon and Microsoft each rose about 1% (Wall Street Journal, IBD).

For the third quarter the S&P 500 gained 2% and the Nasdaq Composite 2.5%, while the Dow fell 2.7%. Both indexes are on course for a fourth straight year of double-digit gains, a first since the late 1990s. Whether rates get in the way is today's question (Wall Street Journal).

What to do today — three things

Here is what to do now, before the Thursday open in New York.

① Micron beat. Handle memory stocks after you see the opening reaction. Revenue and earnings both topped fiscal fourth-quarter forecasts and the company raised its outlook. But the market had already expected a big beat. The stock was flat at $1,065.11 on the 30th and edged higher after hours (IBD, 11:04 p.m. ET, Sept 30).

② Rates are today's biggest variable. Second-quarter growth was revised up to 2.2% from 1.5%, and August PCE prices rose 3.4% from a year earlier. Those numbers say the economy is not cooling, so the worry about another hike remains. Even so, the odds of an October hike fell to 37% from 50.9% a day earlier (Wall Street Journal, IBD).

③ Diesel is at a record. Weigh refiners against the threat of an export ban. US refineries are running near 97% of capacity. Five weeks before the midterms President Trump has floated a ban on diesel exports, which could crush refining margins (Wall Street Journal).

So what do we do: Well grounded today: AMD, Palo Alto Networks and Snowflake. Money went to technology even as yields rose. If the 10-year closes above 5.3%, that trade is the first to be tested.

Theme 1 — AI server orders that lifted GDP

Consumer spending and investment drove the upward revision to second-quarter growth. The Wall Street Journal noted that the bigger investment figure shows how much AI infrastructure is carrying the economy. On the 30th that spending showed up as an order at Hewlett Packard Enterprise.

Cloud provider Vultr placed a large order for HPE systems built on AMD's new Helios rack, and HPE raised its networking revenue outlook (IBD). The same day Logitech's chief executive described scouring the world for chips as AI demand squeezes supply (Wall Street Journal).

Theme 2 — Software that rose as yields climbed

Rising yields usually hit growth stocks first, because they shrink the value of profits far in the future. The 30th went the other way: the software ETF (IGV) rose 1.2%, and Microsoft, Palo Alto Networks and Snowflake all gained (IBD).

The Wall Street Journal credits strong corporate earnings for how well stocks have absorbed higher yields. But Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has said financial conditions are not restrictive. If the Fed tightens further, these are the names most exposed.

Theme 3 — Record diesel, and the threat of an export ban

US diesel prices hit all-time highs in recent weeks, and US refineries are running at about 97% of capacity. S&P Global estimates diesel exports brought in $25 billion between June 13 and September 11. Brent crude sits near $100 a barrel (Wall Street Journal).

The risk is policy. President Trump has floated a diesel export ban and asked Xi Jinping to ship more refined products. S&P Global reckons a ban would crush refining margins and cut US refinery runs by 12%. Chinese refiners run at 75%, so there is spare capacity, but extra fourth-quarter exports are estimated at only about 100,000 barrels a day (Wall Street Journal).

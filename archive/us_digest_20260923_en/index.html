What to do today
1. Memory chips cleared their short-term highs, on lighter than usual volume.
Micron rose 5.00% to $1,096.16 and Sandisk 6.82% to $1,887.04. Both carry a Zeki Strength of 97. Their volume was 0.84 and 0.91 times the 50-day average: the price moved first and the trading has not caught up. If you buy, buy small.
2. Wealth managers and brokers sold off on AI agent fears. Wait a day before buying the dip.
The rapid spread of Meta's AI agent app Muse raised fears it could take over work done by wealth managers, brokers and insurers (WSJ). Charles Schwab fell 6.11%, LPL Financial 7.45% and Ameriprise 4.36%, each on more than twice its 50-day average volume. Selling with that much weight is a reason to watch one more day.
3. Crude has fallen 10.6% in five sessions. The edge sits with companies that buy fuel.
Expiring October WTI fell 1.2% to $94.59 a barrel, and November, now the front month, slipped below $90. President Trump threatened Iran at the United Nations but later called a meeting with Iranian representatives "very productive". He is also weighing limits on diesel exports. (IBD · WSJ)
So what do we do: If you add today, add in memory chips and diagnostics, and add smaller than usual. Financials can wait until the selling volume dies down.

What it is. A record of the companies we found in the newspapers and named in the digest, marked at that day's close, then counted as the price moves. Grouped by how firm the evidence was.
What it is for. To keep a number on whether names picked from the news actually perform. Each name is recorded on the day it ran, so the winners cannot be chosen afterwards.
What it is not. Not a recommendation, not something we bought, and not yet a verdict. That comes once, from a pre-registered test, when both markets together reach 60 cases.
How long a record. Most of these numbers cover a few days. Prices rise for a few days and fall for a few days; the shorter the window, the more flattering — or the more punishing — it looks.

What You Have To Know Today
Crude rose further and the long end did not follow
Brent rose 1.04% to $95.63 a barrel at the 9/2 New York close and WTI rose 0.88% to $91.01. The US 10-year, however, finished at 4.80% and the 30-year at 5.27%, both unchanged from the day before. Long rates that had been climbing with crude for several sessions stopped in a single session. What repriced instead was the yen: it reached a one-month high as expectations of Bank of Japan increases built, and that lifts the cost of funding worldwide. Bonds caught their breath on both sides of the Atlantic, and in equities the Russell 2000, the index carrying the most borrowing cost, rose the most at 1.13%.
Why. What divides the coverage is what this pause means. Bloomberg locates the rise in rates in the volume of government issuance; Investor's Business Daily frames it as the Treasury secretary's response to the bond market alongside borrowing to fund artificial-intelligence buildout. Money Today reads the same yields as a de-rating of AI equities. If the first is right, issuance management can ease it. If the second is right, it is a discount-rate problem inside the most crowded trade in the world. One session's pause has not yet erased either explanation.
What follows. The week's axis is the US August employment report on 9/4 at 08:30 ET. That number either confirms the 9/2 pause or reverses it. A strong labor print hardens the case for higher policy rates and pushes the long end up once more; a weak one extends the reprieve in bonds. The European Central Bank decision on 9/10 follows.
For what we own: For anything that earns its profit furthest out, a day when long rates stood still is a reprieve rather than a repair. If you hold small caps or growth names, there is one thing to check: whether the Russell 2000 keeps leading the cap-weighted indexes for more than a session. Watch the other direction too. Crude is still rising, and when yen funding gets dearer it is leveraged money that shrinks first. A strong employment print on 9/4 could end this pause in a day, and adding size before it amounts to a bet on one statistical release.

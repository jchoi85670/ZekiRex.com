One thing, in depth
The 10-year yield clears 5% and the selling reaches Tokyo
In New York on Sept. 23 the 10-year Treasury yield closed above 5% and the 30-year at 5.40%. The S&P 500, the Nasdaq Composite and the Russell 2000 fell together, and the selling carried into the Tokyo bond market.
Why. Views on the cause split: the Financial Times pointed to oil above $100, Bloomberg to inflation and fiscal worries. Higher yields shrink what future profits are worth today, and technology shares and small companies, whose profits sit further out, feel it first.
What follows. Two things carry into the next edition: whether the 10-year holds above 5%, and whether the Russell 2000 climbs back above its 50-day moving average.
For what we own: If you hold rate-sensitive names, look at them now: growth stocks whose profits lie far ahead and small companies with heavy debt come first. Short-term Treasury products work the other way, their return rising if a hike comes. The decision comes at the Oct. 27-28 Fed meeting.

Also worth knowing
Meta plans $145 billion of capital spending · Fortune reported on Sept. 24 that Meta plans to spend $145 billion on capital projects this year, about double 2025. (Fortune)
→ The money goes to servers and storage, but the plan lands with yields at a 19-year high, so the question is whether funding costs slow the pace. If you hold server makers, watch the order backlog in Dell's Nov. 27 results.
Hong Kong's August exports rise 53% · The South China Morning Post reported on Sept. 24 that Hong Kong's August exports rose 53% to HK$667.9 billion, led by demand for AI products. (South China Morning Post)
→ A second strong month after July is evidence that orders for AI hardware have not slowed. If you hold chip or hardware names, watch which shows up in results first: the rate burden or this order flow.
Micron reports Sept. 30 with a short seller and a bull at odds · Investor's Business Daily reported on Sept. 23 that Michael Burry added to a short in Micron, expecting a chip down cycle. MarketWatch wrote that UBS remains upbeat on memory demand. (Investor's Business Daily · MarketWatch)
→ It is the top name on our list. The Sept. 30 report is the first hard number either side gets, and adding heavily before it is a bet on one company's announcement.
Sanders and Casar file a bill to ban superintelligent AI · PBS and NBC reported on Sept. 23 that Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar filed a bill to ban superintelligent AI, with up to 20 years in prison for violations. (PBS NewsHour · NBC News)
→ No vote is scheduled, so the bill does not change AI spending today. Whether it spreads into November midterm pledges is what to watch.
Indian insurers and lenders fall on a commission cap · Bloomberg reported on Sept. 24 that Indian insurers and lenders fell after the insurance regulator proposed a commission cap. The National Stock Exchange of India rose as much as 5.2% on its debut the same day. (Bloomberg · Wall Street Journal)
→ Rising US yields are widening the gap over emerging Asian bonds. If you hold an India fund, note that the regulatory news and the yield gap pushed the same way on the same day.
Korea and the US: Seoul closed on Sept. 23 local time and stays shut through Sept. 27 for Chuseok, so it closed before the bond selling took hold. It takes in the yield spike and the outcome of the US-China summit all at once when it reopens on Sept. 28 local time.

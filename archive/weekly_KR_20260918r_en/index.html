The week's result was settled on its final day. The Seoul benchmark opened on September 14 with a 3.26% fall to 6,684.37, and stayed under pressure through midweek as the Federal Reserve raised its policy rate by a quarter point on September 16 (local time). It then rose 2.66% on September 18 to close at 6,894.23, a gain of 3.14% on the week. What supplied the force on the last day was crude and American long yields falling together. Our simulated portfolio, however, fell 0.12% over the same week, trailing the index by 3.26 percentage points.
The five things that mattered this week
The five events of the week that touched money. Each one runs: what happened, why, and what it means.
1
The Federal Reserve raised rates for the first time in three years
FACT  On September 16 (local time) the American policy band moved from 3.50-3.75% to 3.75-4.00%, a quarter-point increase.
WHY  It was the first increase since July 2023. Chair Kevin Warsh stated that inflation had been too high for too long.
ACTION — When the price of borrowed money rises, companies valued on distant profits are marked down first. If your account leans to growth names, check more often whether this quarter's earnings actually arrive.
2
Crude topped 100 dollars and then gave it back
FACT  A Saudi pipeline disruption pushed crude above 100 dollars a barrel early in the week; West Texas Intermediate ended 5.72% lower at 95.6.
WHY  The move reversed as a truce proposal emerged. As crude fell, the American ten-year yield came back below 5%.
ACTION — For airlines, shipping and refining, where fuel is a large share of cost, earnings depend on whether this retreat holds. For refiners the domestic price ceiling means a fall in international prices does not pass straight to profit.
3
Seoul took the whole week back on its final day
FACT  The index opened the week at 6,684.37 on September 14 and closed at 6,894.23 on September 18, a gain of 3.14%.
WHY  Through Thursday the tally was negative. A single Friday session of 2.66% changed the direction of the week.
ACTION — A weekly figure on its own hides how far the week travelled. If you pick names on a weekly return, look also at how many days produced it.
4
The lead came from chips and electrical equipment
FACT  By industry the week showed telecom equipment up 15.28%, electrical equipment 12.18% and semiconductors and equipment 8.26%.
WHY  The falling side was banks down 3.52%, brokers 3.05% and property insurance 2.80%. The financial groups fell together.
ACTION — This week moved against the common view that higher rates favour banks. If you hold banks and property insurance, then while the lead sits with chips and electrical equipment, look first at loan quality rather than at the policy rate.
5
The currency did not come back
FACT  The won ended September 18 at 1,385.80 per dollar, 2.77% weaker on the week, against 1,345.00 on September 11.
WHY  Three weeks of won strength reversed in a single week, and the dollar had its strongest week since June.
ACTION — If you hold foreign shares measured in won, the currency gain this week may have exceeded the price move itself. For firms that import raw materials it is that much added cost.

The five things to know for next week
Five that follow from this week, each with what to watch.
1
Only three trading days
FACT  The autumn holiday runs from September 24 to 27, and the exchange is closed on the 24th and 25th.
WHY  Trading runs from September 21 to 23, so a week of volume is compressed into three sessions.
ACTION — Thinly traded names can swing more than usual. If you plan to buy just before the break, allow for a wider gap between the price you see and the price you get.
2
Foreign news piles up during the break
FACT  The New York market trades normally on September 24 and 25 (local time). Only Seoul is shut.
WHY  If crude and American yields move during the holiday, the Seoul benchmark cannot respond.
ACTION — Several days of news arrive at once when the market reopens on September 28. Adding heavily just before the break means holding a position you cannot touch for four days.
3
Watch whether crude keeps falling
FACT  This week's advance came from crude and American yields easing together, and whether that condition holds is next week's question.
WHY  If the truce proposal does not hold, crude rises again and yields follow it up.
ACTION — In that case this week's recovery simply unwinds. If you hold both fuel-exposed names such as airlines and shipping and also growth names, one event can hit you twice.
4
Hyundai Marine & Fire sits closest to its stop
FACT  Hyundai Marine & Fire closed September 18 at 48,400 won against a stop at 47,196, leaving 2.55% of room.
WHY  Property insurance fell 2.80% this week. When a whole group falls together, a single name rarely holds up on its own.
ACTION — We do not sell early because room has narrowed; we sell the whole position when the set price is reached. If you hold the same group, it is better to decide your own exit level now.
5
Watch whether the leading groups hold
FACT  Of the 16 names that cleared our standard, six are property insurers and banks, the two groups that fell this week.
WHY  The lead sits with chips and electrical equipment, and our list is not heavily placed there.
ACTION — If leadership does not change, our list may keep trailing the index. We are not changing the rule, and we are telling you the fact as it stands.

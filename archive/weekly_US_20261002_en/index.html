The week in one sentence. Rates pressed on the market all week until Friday's jobs report took some of the weight off. The 10-year US Treasury yield broke above 5.33% intraday on October 1, its highest since 2002. On October 2 the September report showed payrolls up just 29,000, far below forecasts, with unemployment rising to 4.2%. Bets on another Fed hike this month faded and the Nasdaq 100 closed at a record. Over the week the Standard and Poor's 500 added 0.51% and the Nasdaq composite 1.38%, while the Dow lost 0.59%. The winners were chip equipment and optical networking gear; the losers were mortgage REITs, which live on rates.
The five things that mattered this week
The five events of the week that touched money. Each one runs: what happened, why, and what it means.
1
The 10-year breaks its highest level since 2002
FACT  The 10-year Treasury yield topped 5.33% intraday on October 1 and finished the week up 0.04 points at 5.28%. US government bonds had their worst quarter since 1994. (Wall Street Journal, Euronews)
WHY  Yields climbed while oil stayed high, and the UK 30-year reached 6%, its highest since 1998. This was not a US story alone; long government debt sold off across the developed world. (The Guardian)
ACTION — If you hold heavily indebted companies or rate-sensitive names, Friday's relief is too early to read as a turn. Even after a weak jobs report the 10-year stayed above 5%, so the cost of money has not come down.
2
September payrolls of 29,000 cut October hike bets
FACT  Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000 in September against forecasts of 84,000 to 90,000. Unemployment rose to 4.2% and the prior two months were revised down. (Bloomberg, CNBC)
WHY  The Fed raised rates in September and markets had feared a back-to-back move in October. The weak report eased that fear, and the dollar had its worst day in a month on Friday. (Bloomberg)
ACTION — A pause in hikes is relief for growth stocks, but weak hiring also means weaker spending. Consumer confidence fell to 81.9 on September 29, the lowest since 2014. (CNBC) Which of those two readings wins is not settled yet.
3
Chip equipment and optical gear lead the week
FACT  Semiconductor equipment rose 9.59% and communication equipment 8.99%, first and second of 105 groups. Applied Optoelectronics gained 19.39%, Coherent 19.33% and Lumentum 17.81%.
WHY  Nvidia added a record $150 billion to its buyback. (Wall Street Journal, New York Times) Micron said data center revenue grew elevenfold. (CNBC) Whatever goes into building AI data centers rose together.
ACTION — Micron's shares fell the day after strong results, a sign that expectations were already in the price. If you hold this theme, watch whether good numbers increasingly fail to lift the stock.
4
Rate-dependent groups and big drugmakers fall
FACT  Mortgage REITs fell 7.28%, last of 105 groups. Large drugmakers lost 3.36% and biotechnology 3.21%. Fair Isaac fell 21.36%.
WHY  Mortgage REITs hold home-loan bonds on borrowed money, so rising long rates hit them directly. Fair Isaac was sold after the Federal Housing Finance Agency proposed changing how credit scores are priced, a threat to its near-monopoly. (Edaily, CNBC)
ACTION — If you hold mortgage REITs for the dividend, watch long rates before the yield. While the 10-year stays above 5%, losses in this group can continue.
5
All three of our names rise; the book lands between the indexes
FACT  Marathon Petroleum rose 8.45%, HF Sinclair 6.75% and Dell Technologies 3.51%. The book finished the week up 1.20%.
WHY  Oil refining rose 5.96%, fourth among all groups. Because most of the book sits in cash, only part of the stocks' gains reached the account, which is why it finished ahead of the Standard and Poor's 500 but behind QQQ.
ACTION — The two refiners move together. On October 2 the G7 agreed to release 100 million barrels of diesel and crude from reserves; if diesel prices fall, refining margins narrow and both can fall at once.

The five things to know for next week
Five that follow from this week, each with what to watch.
1
Fed minutes: the October hike gets re-weighed
FACT  Minutes of the Fed's September meeting are due next week; the report did not give the day. (IBD) New York Fed President Williams said on September 29 there is no urgency for the next hike. (Reuters)
WHY  After a weak jobs report, the question is how worried members already were about hiring. If many wanted more hikes, Friday's relief will be short.
ACTION — If you hold rate-sensitive names, watch yields around the release. A return to 5.33% on the 10-year would shake this week's growth winners first.
2
The diesel release: how much refining margin it takes
FACT  On October 2 the G7 agreed to release 100 million barrels of diesel and crude from reserves, starting immediately; oil fell more than 4% on the news. (MarketWatch, South China Morning Post)
WHY  Refiners buy crude and sell diesel and gasoline. A diesel release pushes product prices down faster than crude, narrowing the margin that drove refiners higher this week.
ACTION — Two of our three holdings are refiners. If you carry energy exposure, track the gap between diesel and crude rather than the barrel itself.
3
October 5, Supreme Court climate case: oil companies' liability
FACT  On October 5 the Supreme Court hears a case that could decide whether state-court climate damage suits against oil companies go ahead, with billions of dollars at stake across dozens of cases. (Axios, Reuters)
WHY  No ruling comes on the day of argument, but the justices' questions let the market re-price how likely the suits are to proceed.
ACTION — If you hold oil or refining names, this is a risk on a longer clock than earnings. Rather than reacting to one day's move, note when a ruling is due.
4
October 8, Samsung Electronics preliminary results: memory demand again
FACT  Samsung Electronics reports preliminary third-quarter results in Seoul on October 8, local time. Micron said on September 30 that data center revenue grew elevenfold. (CNBC)
WHY  Samsung is the world's largest memory maker. Its numbers show whether Micron's quarter was one company's story or the whole industry's.
ACTION — If you hold memory or chip-equipment names, this is the first number that tests this week's rally. Micron already showed how much is in the price.
5
October 6, Paramount-Warner closes: the price of big debt
FACT  Paramount's takeover of Warner Bros. is expected to close on October 6. (Fortune) The same day Twilio replaces Warner Bros. Discovery in the Standard and Poor's 500. (IBD, MarketWatch)
WHY  Paramount sold $30 billion of bonds against $109 billion of orders, yet the bonds fell once trading began. (Bloomberg) There is plenty of money to lend, but it wants a higher rate.
ACTION — If you hold companies funding big deals or capital spending with debt, look at what they pay to borrow. With the 10-year above 5%, the same debt costs more.
